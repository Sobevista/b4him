## CONFIRMED FINDINGS

### 1. Navigation

**Visible navigation beats hidden.** NN/g + WhatUsersDo, 2016, n=179, 6 sites, desktop and smartphone: *"a more than 20% drop in discoverability on sites with hidden navigation, compared with sites with visible or combo navigation."* Navigation-use rates — mobile 57% hidden vs 86% combo; desktop 27% hidden vs 48% visible vs 50% combo. `[high]` — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/

**NN/g's current position is blunt.** Menu-Design Checklist (Laubheimer, 2024-06-07), verbatim: *"the hamburger menu (or any form of hiding the navigation categories under a single menu) is not appropriate for desktop"* and *"Menus shouldn't be hidden when you have plenty of space to display them."* `[high]` — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/menu-design/

**The hamburger is recognized but not universal.** NN/g documents a participant who scrolled, returned to top, searched repeatedly, and abandoned the task without ever trying the menu. A June 2025 NN/g follow-up found icon *recognition* has improved but explicitly declines to overturn the usability finding: *"increased recognizability doesn't necessarily mean that task times or success rates for hamburger-menu tasks have changed."* Live position: usable but not universal — *"if you don't need to hide navigation, don't."* `[high]`

**Labels must be plainly descriptive, even at the cost of being boring.** Guideline 7 verbatim: *"Menus are not the place to get cute with made-up words, internal jargon, or abstract high-level categorization."* Category-names article (2013, updated 2024-10-11): *"Your navigation labels should be easily understood, even if they feel boring"*; *"Category and link labels should tell users what will happen when they click on them."* Familiar "About Us" is contrasted favourably against invented "Company Experience." `[high]` — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/category-names-suck/

> **The finding that hits this build hardest:** NN/g's own material explicitly condemns vague *verbs* as nav labels, naming **"Explore, Discover, Learn, Partner."** That indicts not just "The Work" but **"Learn," "Play," and "Watch"** — i.e. three of the four shelf labels already shipped on the landing page.

**But the verdict on any specific label is reasoned application, not a research finding.** NN/g never evaluated these labels, and its remedy for a brand-mandated label is to explain it in adjacent copy. Honest framing: *flagged for renaming or user-testing*, not *proven defective*. NN/g prescribes card sorting or tree testing. `[medium]`

**Sticky headers are conditional.** Guideline 15 is worded *"Consider Sticky (or Partially Sticky) Menus for Long Pages"* — the hedge is deliberate against sibling guidelines' imperatives. Linked research reports ~22% navigation time savings, against a content-to-chrome tradeoff that costs most on mobile. `[high]`

### 2. Text alignment

**Justified text is an explicit accessibility failure; ragged-right satisfies the criterion.** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.8 Visual Presentation requires that *"Text is not justified (aligned to both the left and the right margins)"*; failure technique F88 documents justification as a failure; sufficient technique G169 confirms one-sided alignment passes. Unchanged from WCAG 2.0 (2008) through 2.2. `[high]` — https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html

Three qualifications the verifiers insisted on: this is **Level AAA**, not the AA standard that is the practical/legal target; the strict failure is justified text *with no mechanism to unjustify it*; and 1.4.8 bundles five requirements, so left alignment alone does not confer conformance.

**W3C's stated rationale is scoped, and the general-population evidence is contested.** W3C: *"rivers of white space… making reading difficult and in some cases impossible"* — but scoped to *"People with certain cognitive disabilities."* A verifier surfaced real dissent: a 2017 study where sloppy unhyphenated justification read slower, a **larger follow-up finding little or no difference** (even slightly faster for hyphenated justified text), and a 1998 study finding no significant difference. `[medium]`

**Line spacing:** SC 1.4.8 specifies ≥1.5 within paragraphs, paragraph spacing ≥1.5× line spacing — but W3C states the mechanism *"can be provided by the browser… Content is not required to provide the mechanism."* The AA criterion SC 1.4.12 Text Spacing uses **different numbers** (line height ≥1.5× font size, paragraph spacing ≥2× font size) and is about *tolerating user override*, not authoring defaults. Conflating the two is the easy error. `[high]`

**The dyslexia study everyone cites in alignment arguments never tested alignment.** Rello & Baeza-Yates, *Universal Access in the Information Society* 16:29–49. A verifier pulled the full PDF and grepped it: the manipulated variables were font/background colour, font size, column width, and character/line/paragraph spacing. Alignment was a *held-constant design default*, justified by citing third parties. **Centered text is never mentioned anywhere in the paper.** `[high]` — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-015-0438-8

### 3. Footers

**Always carry utility links, top-of-page duplication notwithstanding.** NN/g verbatim: *"Whether you have links to these utility items at the top of your page or not, always include them in the footer as well."* On risk: footers *"will never get in the way of users who get their needs satisfied higher up on the page"* — *"a cost-free addition to the user experience. It can help, but it can't hurt."* ~80% of sites put privacy policies in the footer. `[high]` — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/

**Fat footer / doormat navigation is scoped, not blanket.** NN/g's *"Use for:"* line: *"sites with long pages, especially if the global navigation is unavailable while at the bottom of the page."* The "can't hurt" claim carries an explicit exception for *"overly bloated footers that could delay page download or rendering."* Separate "false bottom" research shows footer content can go unseen entirely — an argument that footers may be *ineffective*, not that they interfere. `[high]`

### 4. Legal — only two things survived

**The 2024 DOJ web rule does not bind this site.** It amends 28 CFR Part 35 — ADA **Title II** — covering *"all state and local governments… as well as special purpose districts, Amtrak, and other commuter authorities."* Nonprofits appear only via the contractor pathway. **Time-sensitive:** DOJ issued an interim final rule published 2026-04-20 extending compliance dates (50,000+ population entities to 2027-04-26; smaller entities to 2028-04-26). Scope unchanged. `[high]` — https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/

**Not binding ≠ no obligation.** Verifiers flagged this independently and forcefully: private website litigation runs through **ADA Title III**, a separate question this evidence set never reached; and HHS's May 2024 Section 504 rule imposes WCAG 2.1 AA on recipients of HHS federal financial assistance. `[medium]`

**Plausible's technical claim holds; the legal conclusion does not follow.** Verbatim: *"We do not use cookies, we don't generate persistent identifiers and we don't collect or store personal data."* Nobody credible disputes the architecture. The leap to "therefore no consent banner required" is contested — GitHub discussion plausible/analytics#1963 is literally titled *"'No need for cookie banners' might be incorrect,"* and the EDPB position that pseudonymisation is not anonymisation bears on Plausible's `hash(daily_salt + domain + IP + user_agent)`. The salt rotates every 24h, so a strict reader calls it a day-long identifier. Vendor self-description is authoritative for its own architecture, **not** for the legal conclusion. `[medium]` — https://plausible.io/data-policy

## EVIDENCE-QUALITY CAVEATS

- **The load-bearing navigation evidence is a single 2016 study**, n=179, ~30 per site, in a domain where mobile convention has moved a decade. Best available, not replicated fact.
- **The ">20% drop" is an aggregate**, and degradation was *smaller on mobile* than desktop — "over 20% on both" overstates the symmetry. The mobile arm had **no visible-only condition**, so any mobile hidden-vs-visible comparison would be fabricated.
- **Almost all NN/g material is expert-derived guidance from qualitative practice, not controlled experiments with effect sizes.** It belongs in the "well-supported professional convention" bucket. The 2016 study is the quantitative exception.
- One quote in the input set was a paraphrase presented as verbatim; a verifier supplied the real sentence. Corrected above.
