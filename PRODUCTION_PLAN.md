# PRODUCTION PLAN — how work becomes a post, and how we stay ahead of ourselves

*Born 2026-08-16, at Daniel's ask, and his words are the specification:*

> *"We hit a cadence where we build a rung, I red team, and we put into publication process, I
> keep going, but we tier out the delivery so we control 'time' — we will be incredibly ahead
> of our posts."*
>
> *"Review dates for me to review material by, scheduled updates to site, preview of update,
> the whole nine yards… Let's build in maintenance tasks. How often we should be reviewing our
> builds, how often should we make tweaks."*

---

## 1. The one idea this whole document exists to protect

**Production runs ahead of publication, and the gap between them is the asset.**

Building a rung takes a day. Publishing its lessons takes seven weeks at two posts a week. If
those two rates are coupled, the site goes quiet the moment life gets busy — which is exactly
when it looks most like abandonment. **Decoupling them is what "controls time."**

So the health metric of this system is not pages published. It is **RUNWAY**:

> **RUNWAY = how many weeks the site could keep posting on its normal cadence if all building
> stopped today.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target runway** | **6 weeks** — comfortable; a bad fortnight costs nothing |
| **Floor** | **3 weeks** — below this, stop opening new rungs and drain the queue |
| **Ceiling** | **none** — being far ahead is the goal, not a problem to correct |

**A build session that does not add runway is not finished.** That is the whole cadence Daniel
described, stated as a number a checker can read.

## 2. The seven stages

Every piece — a lesson, an exercise page, a rung write-up — moves through these in order. **No
stage is skipped, and each has exactly one owner.**

| # | Stage | Owner | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **SOURCE** | build session | raw material exists (`FINDINGS.md`, a curriculum doc, a lap record) |
| 1 | **DRAFTED** | Claude | page written, genericized per **D11**, **gate block attached** if cyber |
| 2 | **REVIEWED** | *the other seat* | **red never clears red** (`PUBLICATION_GATE` §process). For teaching material, an independent genericization grep |
| 3 | **RULED** | **Daniel** | per-piece approval. Never inherited from a previous yes |
| 4 | **PREVIEWED** | **Daniel** | he has seen it **rendered**, not the markdown. A page reads differently than its source |
| 5 | **SCHEDULED** | the board | has a publish date on a real slot |
| 6 | **PUBLISHED** | — | live, and listed in `PUBLICATION_GATE.md`'s table with its gate block |
| 7 | **MAINTAINED** | cadence | re-checked on schedule after going live (§5) |

**Stages 3 and 4 are both Daniel's and they are deliberately separate.** Approving the content
and approving the page are different judgements; collapsing them is how a good lesson ships
looking wrong.

> **AMENDED 2026-10-13 (ledger E00472), in Daniel's words:** *"every release day we should have
> content that should be updated. You and I need to continue working and make the extraction,
> updating, and approval of automatic"* — asked which meaning, he chose **"One tap, auto after."**
> So stages 3 and 4 are still his and still both judged, but on **one tap**: he is shown the
> words and the rendered proof together (a tile in the harness app, `boards/APPROVALS_OWED.md`,
> carrying the proof link and the commit), and Approve is RULED + PREVIEWED on exactly the bytes
> at that commit. Everything before the tap (extract, draft, independent review, render proof)
> and after it (pin, schedule, publish, verify live) runs without him. He is the only gate.

## 3. The calendar — dates, deadlines, and what happens when one is missed

**Cadence (E00568, superseding D15's two a week):** **one page per week, on Tuesday**, hard cap of
one in any rolling 7-day window even when a batch is ready. Daniel, 2026-10-15: "once weekly is the
tempo on Tuesdays"; raised again only after a review shows production hitting its target.

**The review deadline is T-7.** A piece must be at stage **4 (PREVIEWED)** no later than **seven
days before its slot**. That is not bureaucracy — it is what makes the schedule survivable. A
piece needing Daniel's eye 24 hours before a Tuesday makes the Tuesday depend on his calendar,
and the whole point is that it does not.

**If a slot has no eligible piece, THE SLOT SKIPS AND THE SKIP IS RECORDED.**

- It is **not** backfilled with something unready.
- It is **not** silently dropped — the board prints skipped slots with the reason, because a
  silent skip is indistinguishable from a schedule nobody is running. *(No silent caps.)*
- Two consecutive skips is a **signal about runway**, not about the calendar.

**Nothing publishes that Daniel has not previewed.** ~~There is no auto-publish in this system
and there will not be one.~~ *Superseded 2026-10-13 by E00472:* publishing on the slot is
automatic (`tools/release.py`, Tuesdays since E00568), and it can only ever publish the exact bytes his tap
pinned (`approved_blobs`, plus the lessons-index furniture and entry text). Any edit after the
tap pulls the piece back to him. Every run writes a line to `release_log.jsonl`; a release day
with no line is a job that did not run, never a quiet success.

## 4. Preview — what "seeing it" means

A preview is the **rendered page on the local machine**, not a diff and not a description.

```
py -m http.server 8903 --directory .    →    http://127.0.0.1:8903/lessons/<slug>/
```

**What Daniel is looking for at stage 4, and it is a different list than stage 3:**

1. Does it **look** like the other lessons? (D12 normalised all of them to one template on
   purpose — a page that looks bespoke reads as a mistake.)
2. Does the **voice** survive rendering? First person, warm, fumbling included. The About draft
   is the reference.
3. Do the **links** work, including the ones into other lessons?
4. Is there anything on the page he would not say out loud to the person it is about?

**A preview that finds nothing is still a pass, and it is recorded as one** — so that "he
previewed it" is a fact on the board rather than an assumption.

## 5. Maintenance — the part that has no deadline and therefore never happens

*Daniel: "Let's build in maintenance tasks… not specifics, a plan."* So these are cadences with
owners, and the board raises them; what to actually change is a judgement each time.

| What | How often | Why this interval |
|---|---|---|
| **Every published page** | **quarterly** | links rot, facts age, and a later ruling can quietly supersede a published claim. The gate's own history table is what makes this checkable |
| **The publication gate itself** | **on any refusal, else every 6 months** | a refusal is a finding about our own standard (`PUBLICATION_GATE` step 5) and is the best trigger there is |
| **The cadence and runway** | **monthly** | is one a week still right (E00568: raise only after review), is the runway inside its band, are slots skipping |
| **A rung's material** | **when the rung closes, and once more 30 days later** | the second look is the one that catches what only reads wrong after the excitement has worn off |
| **Genericization sweep across the whole site** | **quarterly** | D11's rationale — *consistency lets us track deviation* — only works if the sweep is repeated. One real name appearing anywhere is then an anomaly, and anomalies need a baseline |
| **This plan** | **every 6 months, or when a cadence changes** | a plan nobody re-reads becomes fiction on a schedule |

**Maintenance findings do not jump the queue.** They enter at stage 0 like everything else,
unless the finding is a correction to something already public — **corrections publish
immediately and outside the cadence, because the cap exists to stop noise, not to delay a fix.**

## 6. The cog

*Per `PLUMBING_CONTRACT`: the plan ships with its pipe, simultaneously, or it is a document
pretending to be a system.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **State** | `production_queue.jsonl` — one line per piece, its stage, its dates. The only home |
| **Generator** | `tools/production_board.py` — computes slots, deadlines, runway, overdue and maintenance-due |
| **Surface** | `PRODUCTION_BOARD.md` — generated, **never hand-edited; re-run the generator** |
| **Cadence** | **weekly**, and on demand before any publishing sitting |
| **Approval** | `tools/approvals.py` — `offer` puts a tile in the app; `apply` turns his tap into a racked row (pinned bytes, `approved/<id>` tag) |
| **Release** | `tools/release.py` — on the slot, writes the pinned bytes to main, marks PUBLISHED, regenerates the index and board, pushes, checks the live bytes. Attacked by an independent breaker before use (`tools/test_release_breaker.py`) |

**What the board is allowed to do:** report state, compute dates, name what is blocked and on
whom, and print skipped slots with reasons.

**What it may never do:** publish anything, move a piece to a later stage on its own, or infer
that Daniel reviewed something because time passed. **Stages 3 and 4 advance only by his
explicit word**, and the board records who moved each piece and when.

**It sets no quality bar and scores no page.** Runway has a floor because Daniel set one; there
is no threshold anywhere in this system that a machine invented.
